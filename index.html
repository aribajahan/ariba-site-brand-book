Hover — motion matches destination

A hover tells you what an element does. Whether it moves, and how, follows one question: is there somewhere to go — and is it a card or an image grid.

1

Clickable cards lift. Offer cards and buttons rise on a growing shadow — translateY(−6px), a faint resting shadow that deepens rather than popping, the lift on the GPU and the shadow faded via opacity so it stays smooth even on a heavy page. Class: .offer-lift.

2

Clickable image mosaics zoom. Tight image grids — the Unmissables tiles, the Cognitive Endurance block, the press grid — scale the photo inside its frame (scale(1.06), ~550ms) instead of lifting. A lift collides with its neighbors in a near-gapless grid; a zoom stays inside the frame.

3

Non-clickable cards move in place. A card with no destination never lifts — that would promise a click that isn't there. The scattered quest cards straighten as you look at them; the case-study cards peek, a slight tilt hinting the flip on the back.

The marks

Tape & highlighter.

Two hand-made marks, borrowed from the Unmissables brand book. They do different jobs and follow different rules — the discipline is what keeps them from turning into decoration.

Tape — labels a thing

Case Study 1 Immigrating from Bangladesh Podcast Ambition Sprint
1

Tape labels a thing. Tags, categories, section markers, offer titles — a piece of tape stuck on to name something.

2

Torn at the ends, straight top and bottom. Real tape comes off a roll: clean long edges, an uneven rip where you tear it. Not rough on every side.

3

Rotated ~1.5°; the color carries meaning. Pink = professional (work, case studies, offers). Orange = personal (the life-story timeline, side-quests). Tennis = Unmissables. Cream only as a fallback on a dark or saturated ground where those would wash out or clash. Never yellow — that's the highlighter. Text stays crisp; the torn edge is a layer behind it.

Highlighter — marks an owned idea (hover to draw)

The lens for organizations is Experience-Led Growth. For people it's Cognitive Endurance — keeping your judgment your own as AI offers to do more of the thinking.

On red, the fill goes translucent: let's build something unmissable.

1

Marks an idea you're claiming, not a word you're stressing. Owned IP (Experience-Led Growth, Cognitive Endurance, unmissable) and signature claims. The test: would you want it screenshotted and attributed to you?

2

A lone word is a glitch; a whole line can stand alone. One highlighted word reads like a stray mark someone forgot to delete — so word-marks come in twos or not at all. A highlighted full line reads as deliberate emphasis, like underlining a sentence, and is fine on its own.

3

Never adjacent, ~1–2 per page. One per section at most, never two in a sentence or in neighboring sentences. A page full of highlights is a coloring book.

4

Always yellow — warm, never neon, never pink. 52% on cream. On red it layers the same yellow over an opaque cream base so the red can't bleed through and mud it — a yellow mark on paper. Staying yellow keeps the highlighter one recognizable thing (pink would also collide with the tape). A little gap sits before a mid-sentence mark so it doesn't crowd the word before it.

Both edges are self-contained SVG displacement masks — no images, no library. Tape and highlighter live on aribajahan.com.

Navigation

The floating nav.

Two objects float over the hero and recolor to whatever's behind them, instead of sitting in a full-width bar. The hover is the distinctive part: each letter fills with red from the top down.

How it behaves

1

Floating, not a bar. Wordmark on the left, links on the right, nothing spanning the width, so the hero fills the whole top of the screen.

2

Recolors to the ground. Cream text over dark and red, charcoal over cream. It reads the background behind it as you scroll.

3

Hides on scroll down, returns on scroll up or pause. It stays out of the way while you read and comes back when you scroll up.

4

Bare over the hero, a faint box past it. Nothing behind the links over the hero; past it a faint tinted panel appears — defined by fill, not a border. It just contrasts the ground: a hair darker on cream, lighter on dark, deeper on the red band (never a black slab).

5

Ink-pour hover. Each link fills from the top down, staggered letter by letter, with a rippled edge — cherish everywhere, and charcoal over the red band (cherish would vanish into the red).

6

Mobile is a full-screen menu. Wordmark and a burger; tapping opens a charcoal panel with the links stacked large.

Live on aribajahan.com. The ink pour is an animated clip-path, with no library.

The working rules

Building a new page.

The system in seven moves. Follow them and a new page matches the site without re-deriving a single decision.

Spacing spec

TokenValueWhere it's used
Section max-width1400pxCentered. Shared by every section.
Section padding-xclamp(24, 5vw, 80)pxThe formula that aligns all left edges.
Tier A top padding120pxAny section opening with eyebrow + headline. No exceptions.
Tier B top padding40–90pxUtility rows: marquees, stat strips, dividers.
Card grid gap24pxCards, offers, testimonials.
Photo-grid gap3–4pxPhoto grids and color-blocked panels.
1

Copy nav + footer verbatim. They're fixed. Never rebuild them per page.

2

Rotate the three grounds. Cream, charcoal, red — never more than two same-tone sections in a row.

3

Match the max-width and padding rhythm so nav, hero and sections all align on the left.

4

Use the image-slot for every photo — never a flat background-image div — so images stay swappable in the Studio.

5

Reach for tape only on a real "look here" moment. Don't default to it everywhere.

6

Pair every hover with a press state. Nothing gives feedback on hover alone.

7

16px body floor, fixed type scale. Sizes are decisions. Don't reintroduce a clamp on H2.
